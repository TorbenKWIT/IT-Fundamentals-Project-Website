Use case Commonly used to store and distribute music, movies, software, and other digital files. It can also be used for backups and archiving information. Examples of optical storage include CDs, DVDs, and Blu-ray discs.
Performance Faster than floppy disks but slower than modern SSDs and hard drives. Speed varies across the disc.

CD:
Capacity – 650MB-700MB depending on the type of CD
1x Speed – 0.15 MB/s
Read Speed - 4-8 MB/s (up to 52x)
Avg access time – 80-120 ms

DVD:
Capacity – 4.7GB-17GB depending on the type of DVD
1x Speed – 1.4 MB/s
Read Speed - 10-22 MB/s (up to 16x)
Avg access time – 100-150 ms

Blu-ray:
Capacity – 25GB-128GB depending on the type of disc
1x Speed – 4.5 MB/s
Avg access time – 150-250 ms
Data Longevity Optical discs can last anywhere from 5 years to a century, depending on the disk’s quality, type, and storage conditions.
How data is stored Data is stored as a physical pattern along single spiral travel track, which a laser reads by detecting differences in reflected light. On pressed discs (commercial movies, music CDs, game discs), the spiral is made from “pits” and “lands”. pits are tiny physical indentations molded into the disc. Lands are the flat areas between the pits
