Magnetic Tape

Use case Tape (mostly LTO, Linear Tape-Open, these days) is still used a lot in enterprise IT. It’s slow to reach any one file, but it’s very cheap per terabyte, lasts a long time, and draws no power while it sits on a shelf. So, it’s used wherever data has to be kept but is rarely read. It’s best used for long term archiving.
Performance Tape is fast at reading or writing a long stream of data in one go (about 400 MB/s, similar to or faster than a hard drive). It is very slow at finding one specific file, because the machine has to wind through the tape like a cassette. That can take 30 seconds to a minute or more. This is why tape suits data that is rarely accessed.
Data Longevity Tape can last about 30 years or more when stored properly in a cool, dry place, which is far longer than most other storage. It uses no power while sitting on a shelf, and tapes can be stored away from the drive. They should still be checked every few years and eventually copied onto new tapes.
How data is stored Tape is a long, thin ribbon coated with tiny magnetic particles, wound on a reel inside a cartridge. A read/write head passes over the ribbon and magnetizes the particles in one of two directions, which represent the 1s and 0s of digital data. Data is written in long parallel lines along the length of the tape, which is why it must be read in order.
Other Information Tape is still used in enterprise environments for long-term data archiving due to its cost-effectiveness and durability.
