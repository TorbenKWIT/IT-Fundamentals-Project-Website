Use case Hard drives are the everyday workhorse for storing large amounts of data cheaply. They are common in desktop computers, external backup drives, network storage, and data centers, where capacity and price matter more than top speed. They suit anything that needs a lot of space at a low cost and doesn’t have to be accessed as quickly as on an SSD, such as photo and video libraries, file servers, and backups.
Performance Hard drives are much faster than tape at finding files (about 10 to 15 milliseconds) and read data at roughly 100 to 200 MB/s. They are slower than SSDs, since the arm has to physically move to the right spot on the disk. They offer a good balance of low cost and large capacity, up to 20+ TB per drive.
Data Longevity Hard drives typically last about 3 to 5 years of regular use, though many last longer. They have moving parts (spinning disks and a moving arm), so they can wear out or break from drops, heat, or age. If left unplugged on a shelf, the data usually lasts several years, but the moving parts may fail when the drive is powered on again, so it’s a good idea to keep backup copies.
How data is stored Inside the drive are one or more spinning disks (platters) coated with a magnetic material. A tiny arm with a read/write head hovers just above the surface, like a record player but without touching it. The head magnetizes very small spots on the disk in one of two directions, which represent the 1s and 0s of digital data. To read the data back, the head detects which direction each spot points.
